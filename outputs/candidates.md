## Validation verdict — PARTIAL PASS (honest)

The pipeline has two halves, and they did not perform equally.

### What worked: the hillshade shows the published complex

In the 600x600 m close-up (`closeup_site_vs_control.png`, top row),
within ~100 m of the published coordinates (18.3354 N, 91.1130 W),
the hillshade shows:

- a sharp-cornered SQUARE platform outline ~40–50 m across at
  approx. 18.3364 N, 91.1143 W (UTM ~699300 E, 2028470 N);
- a rectangular platform outline at approx. 18.3352 N, 91.1114 W
  (UTM ~699600 E, 2028330 N);
- a cluster of small ring/circular features NW of the marker
  (UTM ~699350–699420 E, 2028380–2028420 N), matching the
  "roughly concentric circles or rectangles" description in
  Šprajc (2026) for nested constructions.

That matches the literature description of complex #1 (low,
elongated platforms in concentric rectangles, possible marketplace).
The DTM → hillshade half of the pipeline works: the published site
is visible at the published coordinates.

Also visible: a long straight diagonal line crossing the lower part
of the site window (approx. UTM 699400 E, 2028100 N to 699450 E,
2028200 N). Unidentified — could be an ancient causeway (sacbe) or
a modern track. Not claimed either way.

### What did NOT work: the crude automated screening

The relief-threshold screening (local relief > 0.4 m, components
50–5000 m²) flagged 58 components in the 2x2 km window, 12 within
150 m of the site — but it cannot separate the site from background:

- Pixels above 0.4 m relief: 7.9% in the site window vs 6.0% in a
  control window 800 m away on the same swath. Barely above background.
- Relief maxima are NOT at the site (5.67 m site vs 6.67 m control).
- The relief blobs are mostly ROUND in both windows — natural karst
  mounds, rejolladas, and similar bumps dominate the signal. The
  site's platforms are LOW (0.5–2.4 m mean relief), exactly as the
  literature says, so a height threshold cannot find them.
- The control patch is not a true negative anyway: this landscape
  averages ~55 structures/km² (Auld-Thomas et al. 2024), so the
  "background" is itself full of unrecorded settlement. A control
  comparison cannot prove a detection here.

Conclusion: **visual hillshade inspection works (as the literature
does it); crude threshold screening does not.** Automation would need
shape-based screening (rectilinearity, not height) or a trained
model — e.g. the Q2000 CNN from the 2026 Springer paper (F1 0.89),
which is the proper tool for this job and does not exist in this pilot.
